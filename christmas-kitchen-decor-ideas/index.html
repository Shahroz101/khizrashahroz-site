36 Christmas Kitchen Decor Ideas for the Heart of the Home

Garland over the cabinets, cocoa corners, gingerbread bakeries and island centerpieces — 36 Christmas kitchen ideas that still let you cook.

The kitchen is where everyone ends up during the holidays, so it deserves more than a single wreath on the fridge.

A few well-placed touches, like garland over the cabinets, a styled mug shelf or a gingerbread house on the counter, can change the whole room.

The catch is that a kitchen has to keep working: there is heat, grease, splashes and food. So before the 36 ideas, here is how I decorate one without getting in the way of cooking.

Five Rules for a Kitchen That Still Works

Go vertical first. The tops of cabinets, the windows and the walls are free. Counters aren't, so decorate those last.

Respect the heat. Keep paper, fabric and dry garland well away from burners and the range hood. Wreaths belong above the hood, not over the flame.

Use trays. A tray turns scattered decor into one object you can lift and wipe under in a second.

Light it with batteries. Battery candles and fairy lights mean no outlet hunting and no open flame.

Repeat one color. Red, gold or green. Three repeats at different heights is enough to look planned.

Common Kitchen Mistakes (and Fixes)

Crowding the counters. If you can't chop an onion, remove something.

Hanging things near the stove. Heat and grease ruin decor, and paper can catch fire.

Using food that spoils. Faux peppermints and cookies last all season. Real ones are for the day of the party.

Forgetting to look up. The most dramatic spot in most kitchens is the space above the cabinets.

Start with one idea, one color and the top of the cabinets. You can always add the rest next year.
