25 Christmas Wreath Ideas to Dress Up Every Front Door

Plaid bows, frosted pinecones, gold magnolias, gingerbread and more — 25 Christmas wreath ideas for every door color and porch style.

The wreath is the first thing anyone sees when they walk up to your door.

It takes a few minutes to hang, yet it changes how the whole entrance feels, which makes it the highest-impact piece of holiday decor you can buy.

The best wreaths are built around one strong idea, like a bold plaid bow, a cluster of pinecones or an all-white palette. Here are the basics I check first, then 25 wreaths to borrow from.

Five Things to Check Before You Hang a Wreath

Size. Aim for about two-thirds the width of the door. A small wreath on a big door looks lost.

Height. Hang it at eye level, or a little above. Look at your door from the walkway, not from inside.

Hanger. An over-the-door hanger is easiest. Put a bit of felt on the door side so the paint doesn't scuff.

Weather. Outside, choose outdoor-rated ribbon, shatterproof baubles and a covered porch if you can.

Color. Think about your door. A rich wreath looks best on a pale door, and a pale wreath on a dark one.

1. Put Fullness First

Size and fullness matter more than any single ornament.

A good wreath is about two-thirds the width of your door. This one fills its space with dense evergreen, bright red berries, silver-tipped pinecones and glossy holly leaves, with one long velvet ribbon trailing down a white door.

If yours looks thin, tuck in extra berry picks. It takes two minutes and makes a bigger difference than a new bow.

Not every wreath needs a bow. This one is just layers of pine, fern-like sprigs and big brown pinecones against a black door. The shapes and textures are interesting enough without help.

  • If a cone comes loose, a dab of hot glue fixes it.

8. Keep It Simple With Oranges and Cinnamon

The simplest wreaths often look the best.

Dry your own oranges by slicing them thin and baking at 200°F for two to three hours. Mist them with clear sealer if the wreath will hang outside.

10. Make a Wreath Yourself

A handmade wreath is the most personal one on the door. Round slices of birch form this open ring, with a deep red hydrangea, burlap ribbon and trailing greenery in the lower corner.

Glue the slices to a cardboard ring with hot glue and drill a small hole at the top for twine.

It's light enough for any hook. Keep it indoors or on a covered porch.

Common Wreath Mistakes (and Fixes)

Choosing one that is too small. If in doubt, go bigger.

A flat, floppy bow. Use wired ribbon, make large loops and fluff them.

Too many accents. A single standout detail beats five competing ones.

Forgetting the back of the wreath. Wind can spin it. Wire it to the hanger at two points.

Pick the one that makes you smile on the way to your own front door. That's really the whole point.
