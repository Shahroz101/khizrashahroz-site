33 Vintage Christmas Decor Ideas With Nostalgic Charm

Mercury glass, retro Santas, bottle brush trees, velvet stockings and lit villages — 33 vintage Christmas decor ideas for every room.

Vintage Christmas decor feels warmer than almost anything new off the shelf.

Mercury glass, bottle brush trees, retro Santas and scraps of velvet and plaid all have a glow that fresh-from-the-box sets rarely match.

The good news is that you don't need real antiques to get there. A few well-chosen pieces, styled with restraint, can read as nostalgic as something handed down. Here is how I approach it, followed by 33 ideas for every room.

Five Ways to Get the Vintage Look Without Buying Antiques

Mix old and new. A few thrifted pieces among new ones look collected. A room full of reproductions looks like a catalog.

Limit your materials. Choose two or three, such as mercury glass, plaid and brass, and repeat them everywhere.

Light it warmly. Soft bulbs and candles (or battery ones) do half the work.

Choose patina over perfection. A chipped ornament or a worn sled has more character than a flawless one.

Shop where the old things are. Thrift shops, estate sales and flea markets are the best places for glass ornaments, brass candlesticks, crocks and tins.

1. Trim the Tree With Bulbs Your Grandmother Would Recognize

What makes a tree look vintage? Mostly the glass.

Saturated reds, teals, greens and golds, with warm multicolor lights threaded through, take you straight to a 1960s living room. Modern trees tend to be coordinated. Old ones were gloriously mixed.

So mix your finishes, shiny with matte and glitter, and your sizes, and hang the biggest balls low and near the trunk. One safety note: check any old light strings for frayed cords before plugging them in, or switch to LED.

2. Choose Mercury Glass for a Softer Shine

Real mercury glass was silvered on the inside, which is where that soft, aged shine comes from. Newer pieces copy the look at a fraction of the price.

This tree wears silver and cream teardrops with a few deep red balls, and strands of tinsel tucked into the boughs.

Quick tip: Hang the larger pieces nearer the trunk, where they catch the light and give the tree depth.

And tinsel goes on one strand at a time. Clumps look like a mistake.

4. Light Brass Candlesticks Under a Garland

Brass candlesticks are the cheapest way to make a mantel look inherited.

Tall brass sticks with dark candles stand beneath a gilt mirror here, and a thick garland with red berries drapes across the mantel. Thrift shops sell them for a few dollars. A little polish does the rest. Use tapers in odd numbers, and choose dark red or forest green for an old-fashioned look.

Never leave real flames near dry garland. LED tapers are a safe stand-in if the mantel is crowded.

Common Mistakes (and Fixes)

Buying everything new in one color. It will look flat. Add at least one old, mismatched piece.

Using cool white lights. They make everything look modern. Switch to warm.

Leaving fragile glass at toddler height. Hang old ornaments up high and put sturdy shatterproof ones low.

Forgetting cord safety. Check old light strings for frayed wires and never run cords under rugs.

Use what you love, in small doses. A chipped ornament with a story always beats a perfect new one.
